Frontier models are powerful. Unchecked, they’re a liability.

Anyone can rent a frontier model today. What’s missing is the layer around it — the checking, the cost discipline, and the paper trail. That layer is what Hydra is.

01

Confident, fluent, wrong

A single model will state a wrong figure, a wrong citation, or a wrong summary in perfect prose — and then, asked to review it, will usually approve its own work. Errors survive because nothing independent ever looks.

Why this is fundable: the working method

The output so far was produced with almost no budget — what produced it is a method, and the method scales with resources in a way headcount doesn’t.

Checked at the source

Research cards are written from the paper itself — full text, tables, code, and licence — not from posts about the paper. When a social post overstates a paper (it happens often), the card says so, in those words.

Two rounds, different hands

The Still pipeline runs every item through two rounds: one model writes and reviews, a different model reviews and sorts. Disagreements are recorded, not averaged away.

Claims graded, not amplified

Each finding is graded against the Frontier Map as moves, sharpens, confirms, or claim only. Vendor numbers are labelled vendor-reported until independently reproduced.

Built by someone who has priced real work

A construction-trades background means estimates, scope, and verification are habits, not slogans. On a job site, work gets inspected by someone other than the person who did it. Hydra applies the same rule to AI.
